The Venture

Cooling as a service, delivered through shared infrastructure.

Instead of each building running its own chillers, a district system produces chilled water centrally and pipes it to the buildings it serves.

CenterStream is developing that model in downtown St. Louis. For a building owner it means a different capital and operating decision. For the city it means shared infrastructure. For a venture at this stage it means every customer is a negotiated, multi-party commitment, not a transaction.

Why It Is Long-Cycle

Patience, sequenced.

The sale takes months and years because the decision does. Managing that is a skill in its own right.
A

Many decision-makers

Owners, asset managers, engineers, tenants and lenders each have a say, and each needs a different answer.

B

Infrastructure-scale commitments

Customers weigh existing equipment, existing energy arrangements and capital timing before they move.

C

Public approvals in the loop

City, State and federal stakeholders shape both the project and the customers it can reach.

I have run this kind of work before: securing a federal earmark for a transit center in Cortex, leading approvals and financing for the $50M Merchandise Mart Apartments, and building the public case for a $300M convention hotel. The skill is not closing fast. It is keeping every stakeholder moving in the same direction for as long as it takes.
